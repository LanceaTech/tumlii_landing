How are sleep and reflux linked?

Short answer. They affect each other. Asleep, you swallow less and make less saliva, and your stomach empties more slowly, so acid that comes up stays longer. And in the other direction, sleep loss made the gullet more sensitive to acid.

What the review found

  • People with reflux disease more often have shorter sleep, trouble falling asleep, waking in the night and waking early.
  • Going without sleep made the gullet more sensitive to acid.
  • Of the lifestyle steps, not eating late at night plays a part in less night-time reflux.

What to note about your nights

  • When dinner ended and when you lay down (why the gap matters).
  • Whether you woke with heartburn, a sour taste or a cough, and roughly when.
  • How rested you felt. Then compare the next day's tummy after good and poor nights.

What it can't show

When two things affect each other, a diary can't tell you which to change first. Try the easier one, an earlier dinner, for two weeks and count the nights.

Source

  • Fujiwara Y, Arakawa T, Fass R. Gastroesophageal reflux disease and sleep disturbances. J Gastroenterol, 2012
