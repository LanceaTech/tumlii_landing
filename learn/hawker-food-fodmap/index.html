Where the FODMAPs are in 26 hawker dishes

Short answer. In hawker food the FODMAPs are mostly garlic, onion and shallots in the paste, wheat in yellow noodles, prata and buns, and coconut milk in larger helpings. Rice, bee hoon and kway teow are low on their own. What you usually can't see is the stock and the sauce.

This table is Tumlii's own list of what usually goes into each dish, with the FODMAP groups from published research. It is general: no stall's food was tested, cooks differ, and how much you eat matters.

Asking at the stall

Dietitians of the Singapore Nutrition and Dietetics Association, writing on the Monash FODMAP blog, suggest rice noodles such as bee hoon and kway teow; asking for no garlic and onion in dishes cooked to order; asking for no fried shallots on top and for spring onion greens instead; and going easy on curries, laksa and soups, whose base is usually onion and garlic.

Why the soup matters more than the topping

Fructans dissolve in water, so garlic and onion simmered in a stock leave fructans behind even when you can't see a piece. They don't dissolve in oil.

Before you cut anything

A low FODMAP diet is a short test, best done with a dietitian. If you only want to know whether garlic-heavy meals go with your bad days, a diary that counts both sides is a gentler place to start.
