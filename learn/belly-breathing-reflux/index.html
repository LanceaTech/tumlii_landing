Can belly breathing help with reflux?

Short answer. One small trial says it may. Nineteen people with reflux disease were split into two groups. Those who practised belly-breathing exercises for four weeks had less acid in the gullet and a better quality of life; the other group didn't change. It was a small study, so take it as an encouraging sign.

Why breathing at all?

The diaphragm, the muscle you breathe with, wraps around the valve at the bottom of your gullet and works with it. The idea was to train it.

What was measured

Time the gullet was acid9.1%4.7%

Nine months later, 11 of the 19 were still practising. They used less reflux medicine than before (about 25 mg a week against 98); those who had stopped had no lasting change.

What it can't show

Ten people is few. People who keep up an exercise for nine months may differ in other ways too. Don't change a medicine because of a breathing exercise without asking whoever prescribed it.

A simple way to try it

  1. Sit or lie with one hand on your belly and one on your chest.
  2. Breathe in slowly through your nose so the lower hand rises and the upper one stays still.
  3. Breathe out slowly. Five minutes is plenty.

Note the days you did it and the days you didn't, and count how you felt on each.

Source

  • Eherer AJ et al. Positive effect of abdominal breathing exercise on gastroesophageal reflux disease: a randomized, controlled study. Am J Gastroenterol, 2012
