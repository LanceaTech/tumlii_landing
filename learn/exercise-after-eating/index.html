How long should you wait to exercise after eating?

Short answer. Research gives no exact number of hours. A review found that 30 to 50 in every 100 athletes report tummy trouble during exercise, mostly mild, and that hard effort sends less blood to the gut. About two hours after a main meal is a common rule of thumb before hard exercise; a gentle walk is a different matter.

Why hard exercise and a full stomach don't mix well

During intense exercise, less blood goes to the gut, and more so when you are short of fluid. The review calls this one of the main contributors thought to be behind gut symptoms in sport. Food that empties from the stomach quickly, and drinking enough, go with fewer complaints.

What it can't show

The review looked mainly at endurance athletes. It doesn't give a waiting time, and the "two hours" you see everywhere is a habit passed on, not a measured result. Your own gap may be shorter or longer, and may depend on the meal.

How to find your gap

  • Write down when you finished eating, when you started, how hard it was, and how your tummy was.
  • Keep meals roughly alike while you test: a bowl of porridge and a plate of char kway teow need different gaps.
  • Change only the gap. After eight or ten sessions you can count short gaps against long ones.

Blood in your stool, or severe pain during or after exercise, needs a doctor, not a diary.

Source

  • de Oliveira EP, Burini RC, Jeukendrup A. Gastrointestinal complaints during exercise. Sports Med, 2014
