What is FODMAP, in plain words?

Short answer. FODMAPs are a group of sugars and fibres that the small intestine doesn't absorb well. They draw in water and are fermented by gut bacteria, which can stretch the bowel and goes with bloating, wind and discomfort in some people.

What the letters stand for

GroupWhere you meet it
Oligosaccharides: fructans, GOSonion, garlic, wheat, lentils, beans
Disaccharide: lactosemilk, yogurt, fresh cheese
Monosaccharide: excess fructosemango, apple, honey
Polyols: sorbitol, mannitolmushrooms, stone fruit, cauliflower

What studies found

Cutting back on all of them together, rather than one at a time, eased gut symptoms for many people, and bringing them back brought symptoms back. The amount matters: a food can be low in a small helping and high in a big one, and the same food differs between countries and recipes.

Why do it with a dietitian

The researchers are clear that it needs a dietitian trained in it. It is a short test followed by bringing foods back, not a way to eat for ever.

Where a diary fits

When you bring foods back, one at a time, the diary tells you which and how much. Write down the helping, and keep the rest of the day plain. If you eat out a lot, see FODMAPs in hawker food.
