How to keep a food and symptom diary that actually shows you something

Short answer. Write down the good days as well as the bad ones. A diary that only holds bad days can't show anything, because there is nothing to compare them with. Note each meal and its time, how your tummy felt and when, and a few things besides food (sleep, stress, late meals). Keep going for at least two weeks, then count.

What to write down

  • Each meal and drink, with the time. The dish and roughly how much is enough. "Laksa, large, 8:40 pm" is more use than a perfect list a day late.
  • How you felt, when it started, and how bad, on the same scale every time (say 0 to 3).
  • When you felt fine. One line is enough: "fine all afternoon". This is the line people skip, and the one that matters most.
  • When you went to bed, how you slept, and whether it was a stressful day.
  • Medicines, with the time.

Three mistakes that spoil a diary

1. Only writing on bad days

If you only write down the days you felt bad, every food you eat often will look guilty. Coffee shows up before most of your bad days because you drink it most days. You need the good days with coffee, and the days without it, to tell.

2. Counting a blank day as a good day

A day with nothing written is a day you don't know about. If you count it as fine, the foods from your busy, unwritten days look better than they are. Leave blank days out of the sum.

3. Forgetting what came along with it

The spicy dinner was also the late dinner, after a hard day. When two things nearly always come together, a diary can't separate them. Change one and keep the other the same for a couple of weeks.

How to read it: count, then compare

Take one question at a time. Count how often you were upset with it, and how often without it.

With only three or four meals on one side, leave it. The next one could turn it round.

How long to keep it

At least two weeks, with two weekends in it. Foods you eat once a week need a month or more before there is anything to compare.
